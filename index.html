Extruder for Filament Production from PET Packaging

Hello everyone! Filament prices haven't been pleasing lately at all. It is also frustrating that some parts have to be reprinted multiple times to get the desired results. The process of developing and printing a part can sometimes be quite iterative and meditative. What if you had unlimited plastic? A sort of "superpower" — filament-man? Would this help you in your 3D creative work?

We present to your attention RePet Compact — a compact PET bottle filament extruder!

💡

Hint: Find more videos and tutorials on our YouTube channel

Operating Principle

The extruder operates on the technology of direct transformation of PET bottles into a tape, and then into a filament suitable for 3D printing. This technology is called "pulltrusion", and consists of the fact that the PET bottle does not need to be crushed into crumbs used as raw material, but is simply cut into a tape, heated to 180 degrees, and pulled through a hotend-former using a winch. At such a low temperature, the plastic does not emit an odor, unlike the extrusion technology, which requires melting the material to a fluid state with all ensuing consequences. I wouldn't even call this process extrusion in full, rather — PET conditioning — preparing ready-made plastic for 3D printing.

Unlike industrial plants for plastic production, pulltrusion technology does not require expensive equipment (plastic grinder, pellet screw extruder, etc.) and consumes much less electricity. With this extruder, for example, from a single five-liter bottle you can get 16-19 meters of PET filament. The machine can be installed right on the 3D printer (or nearby) and print directly from the machine's feed spool. The machine operates from a 220V network, and there is also a 12V 4A version for work in, so to speak, "field conditions".

Transformation of Packaging into Tape

Carried out using a bearing-based bottle cutter. At this stage, you can adjust the angle of the bottle (according to its diameter) and the width of the resulting tape using a changable preadjusted tape width guider.

📏

Ribbon width: 2-8 mm

⚙️

Ribbon speed: 4-5 mm/sec

💡

Hint: you can order tape width guider in our store or print it and assemble by your own

Pulltrusion (PET conditioning)

Next, the tape enters the inlet funnel of the hotend-former, where heating and twisting of the tape into a tube takes place. The hotend-former is made from a prepared standard 3D printer nozzle (brass, stainless steel), a standard unmodified heat block, and a standard ceramic heating element. By the way, the width of the tape affects the filling percentage of the tube.

At the same time, the shape of the tube is more reminiscent of the letter "C" (c-type filament). The existing cavity in the filament is compensated by over-extrusion during 3D printing (I set from 130 to 150 percent in the slicer). The resulting filament is wound onto a spool using a reduction gearbox on a stepper motor.

📏

Ribbon width adjust: 6-7 mm

⚙️

Recommended extrusion flow in Slicer: 130% - 150%

Filament Welding

The machine is equipped with a filament welding device. The filament welder consists of two clothespins with aluminum sponges. To heat the filament in the welder, a NiCr wire coil with a cross-section of 0.2 mm is used, with a total thread length of 200 mm, operating from a built-in 12V power supply. The ends of the filament are installed in the clothespins, when the button is pressed, 12V is supplied to the coil, the ends of the filament are heated by the coil, melted, connected together, and formed by one of the clothespins (any) by pushing the welded pair inside the clothespin. The weld seam is cooled by aluminum sponges. Thus, you can weld and accumulate filament on different spools, sorting threads by colors.

The machine operates in a temperature range of 175 °C, and theoretically allows cutting anything that has the shape of a ribbon or a thin-walled cylinder (bottles, cups, pre-prepared tape).

Features of the Applied Raw Materials

During experiments, various types of PET packaging with thick and thin walls were tested. When working with thick-walled containers, it is necessary to simply reduce the width of the tape using the steel limiter on the bottle cutter. Paper labels on the containers must be removed using a heat gun. Plastic labels are easily removed manually. Washing off adhesive residue is not required.

Printing Parameters

In short:

  • Nozzle: 275–285 °C
  • Bed: 85 °C
  • Extrusion (Flow): 130–150%

Printing with this filament is carried out at nozzle temperatures of 270–280 °C and a bed temperature of 85 °C — it is necessary to fine-tune the nozzle temperature based on the ease of pushing the filament through the extruder. A delicate part cooling fan is required during printing; otherwise, if cooling is insufficient, the plastic cools slowly and crystallizes. On larger models, I set the cooling fan within the 20–30% range. Currently, I print on two printers: a FlyingBear Ghost 5 Direct (teflon tube throat) and a Uni printer with a Titan Aero extruder (all metal throat, brass cube). No difference has been noticed so far. I use over-extrusion to compensate for the internal cavity within the filament (130–150%), as the filament is formed in the shape of a tube. An example of printing with this plastic is the machine itself.

There are quite a few scenarios for using pulltrusion machines, as they are very compact and economical (effectively operating a 12V heater at 160–180 °C and 1 stepper motor). Here are some of them:

💡

Printing where access to purchased plastic is difficult, while PET bottles are available almost everywhere!

💡

Saving your time, money, and energy spent on ordering, waiting, and delivering plastic to your printing location. The machine always stands in the lab and produces the required amount of plastic for work

💡

Using plastic obtained from PET containers as draft material during 3D model design iterations (saves $$)

💡

Promoting a culture of plastic consumption.

Economics of Pulltrusion Technology

How much will a spool of filament obtained by drawing PET tape cost us? Let's calculate! One 5-liter water bottle yields 18–19 meters of plastic (c-type filament, 1.6–1.75 mm). A standard spool contains 300 meters of plastic. Thus, 1 spool requires 16 five-liter PET bottles. The average price of a 5-liter PET bottle on platforms like Avito is about 5 rubles. Total: 80 rubles for the raw materials needed to produce 1 spool of plastic. Electricity costs should also be taken into account, which are about 3 times less than 7.5 hours of 3D printer operation — exactly how long it takes to fill one spool with plastic.
